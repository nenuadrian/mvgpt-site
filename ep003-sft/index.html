SFT: fine-tuning a GPT to follow instructions

Our pretrained model learns to write the story an instruction asks for, with a loss on the story alone, and we measure how much better it follows instructions.

Watch on YouTube

A pretrained model is an autocomplete, so in this episode we fine-tune ours to follow instructions, with TinyStories-Instruct, where each story comes with words to use, features and a summary. We mask the loss so the model learns the story and not the instruction, pad batches of different lengths, and fine-tune for ten minutes. Then we measure the model before and after, on how many of the requested words its stories use, and move the run's logs over to TensorBoard.

Where SFT fits

1:37 This is the pipeline behind assistants such as ChatGPT, in a much simpler form. Pretraining on a lot of text gives a model that knows the language, and continues whatever it's given. Supervised fine-tuning then trains it further on examples of an instruction followed by the answer we want, written by people, or here, generated by GPT models. It uses pretraining's objective, next-token prediction with cross-entropy, on different data and with one change to the loss. After that, preference optimisation, such as DPO, teaches it to prefer the better of two answers, and reinforcement learning, such as PPO, trains it to maximise a reward.
