We wrote it in C++, with a heap of the pairs' counts that each merge updates, and tested it against a reference in plain Python, which learns identical merges and gives identical ids. On a megabyte of stories, the C++ learned its merges about one hundred and twenty times faster than Python. The model got a tokenizer on top of it, with no unknown token, and ten minutes of pretraining on the stories in BPE. In bits per character, it ended a few percent behind the word-level model, which doesn't predict spaces and new lines, while BPE gives back each held-out story as it was, along with names that weren't in its training.
