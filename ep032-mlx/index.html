    Our GPT in Apple's MLX vs PyTorch on a Mac

    We port our GPT from PyTorch to MLX, Apple's array library for its own chips, check that it gives the same numbers, and race the two on this Mac at writing and at training.

    Watch on YouTube

    In this episode, we move our GPT from PyTorch to MLX, Apple's array library for Apple silicon. We write the model in MLX, load our PyTorch weights into it by name, and check that it gives the same logits and writes the same story. Then we race the two frameworks on this Mac's GPU, writing a token at a time and training at pretrain.py's batch size, with MLX's compiler and without, train a model in MLX from scratch, and bring it back to PyTorch.

    What MLX is

    2:06 Its documentation calls it a NumPy-like array framework for machine learning on Apple silicon, from Apple's machine learning research team, and its Python side follows NumPy closely. It came out in December twenty twenty-three, and next to PyTorch, two of its ideas were new to me. MLX is lazy, so when you add two arrays, it writes down that it's going to add them, and does the work once something needs the answer, like printing it or calling eval. That lets it see a whole step before it runs it. The other is unified memory, which means the CPU and the GPU on these chips share one memory, and MLX arrays live there. So there's no moving tensors to a device, as with to device in PyTorch, and operations can run on either, on one array, without copying it. Of the two, unified memory is my favourite, as our PyTorch scripts pick a device and move things onto it, and here there's nothing to pick.
