DPO: teaching a GPT from preferences

With direct preference optimisation, our fine-tuned model learns from pairs of its own stories, where the one that uses more of the words asked for is preferred, and we measure what it gains and what it costs.

Watch on YouTube

In this episode, our model learns from preferences, with direct preference optimisation, DPO, which needs no reward model and no reinforcement learning. The fine-tuned model writes four stories per instruction, and code prefers the one that uses the most of the requested words to the one that uses the fewest. We train on 2,000 of these pairs for ten minutes, measure what changes, and run into reward hacking, as the model raises its score by writing longer stories.

Reward hacking

15:05 Our preference rewarded stories for using the words, and DPO raised that score in ways we didn't ask for. That's reward hacking, on a small scale: the score stands in for what we want, a good story that uses the words, and training raises the score by any route it can find, such as writing longer stories, whether or not they're better.
