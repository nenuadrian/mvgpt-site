Long context LLM: stretching RoPE with YaRN

We stretch the rotary embeddings of our web model to read four times its window, four ways and then with fifteen minutes of fine-tuning, and measure the loss by position and a pass key hidden in filler.

Watch on YouTube

Our web model was pretrained on windows of 1,024 tokens, and its loss climbs past them, so in this episode we stretch its rotary embeddings four ways, from position interpolation to YaRN. We measure each on long web pages, by position, against a sliding window, and with the pass key test, a number hidden in filler. YaRN keeps the loss flat out to 4,096 tokens with no training at all, and then we fine-tune on long pages for fifteen minutes with two of the methods.

NTK-aware, and NTK-by-parts

10:11 The other three come from YaRN: Efficient Context Window Extension of Large Language Models, by Bowen Peng and others, also from 2023. Its abstract says that models with rotary embeddings fail to generalise past the length they were trained on. YaRN extends their context with ten times fewer tokens and two and a half times fewer training steps than the methods before it. Section three point one makes the point we just saw: position interpolation stretches all the pairs equally, which removes the high frequencies, the fast pairs that tell nearby tokens apart. So NTK-aware interpolation, first posted on Reddit in 2023 by a user called bloc97, spreads the slowing across the pairs, scaling the high frequencies less and the low frequencies more. Section three point two looks at each pair's wavelength, the tokens it takes to go once round. Pairs with short wavelengths went round many times, so to them, what matters is the distance between tokens. It leaves the pairs that turn more than beta times as they are, and interpolates the ones that turn less than alpha times. With the paper's values for Llama, alpha is one and beta is thirty-two.

11:37 NTK-aware interpolation does it by changing the base of the speeds, the ten thousand.
