    Sparse autoencoder features inside our GPT

    We train a sparse autoencoder on the residual stream of our web model, read what its features fire on, measure what it costs the model, and steer the model's writing with a feature.

    Watch on YouTube

    In this episode, we look inside the GPT we pretrained on web pages in episode nineteen, with a sparse autoencoder. It writes each token's 768 channels in the middle of the model as a sum of 32 directions out of more than twelve thousand, the features, trained on the fly on this Mac. We find the first token of each window behaving very differently, measure what the autoencoder costs the model's loss, read the contexts where features fire, and push the model's writing around with a single feature.

    The idea

    1:55 As it flows through our web model, each token is seven hundred and sixty-eight numbers, the residual stream.
