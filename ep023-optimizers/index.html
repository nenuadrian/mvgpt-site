    What's real, and what's scaled down

    42:00 A study from 2025 ran this kind of race at a larger scale: Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers and Where to Find Them, by Kaiyue Wen and others at Stanford. Its abstract says AdamW has stayed the optimizer of pretraining, despite claims of speedups of one point four to two times. They tuned ten optimizers with care, on models from a hundred million to one point two billion parameters, and found the speedups lower than claimed, falling to one point one times at one point two billion. The fastest, like Muon and Soap, multiply the gradient by matrices, where AdamW, Lion and Sophia scale each weight on its own. Figure one, at the top left, shows the baseline problem: for a model of a hundred million parameters, in a recipe from GPT-3, tuning the learning rate alone gives up to a two times speedup. And on Sophia, the paper notes that its original code base didn't fully shuffle the data, which lowered AdamW's best learning rate there. In their setting, Sophia doesn't offer a significant speedup over AdamW for models under half a billion parameters.

    43:03 Andrej Karpathy's nanochat trains its model with Muon too.     43:39 The method is the one the study used: tune each optimizer, then compare them at their best, at the end of training.
