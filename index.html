    mvGPT: Minimum Viable GPT: A Language Model from Scratch

    Understand language models from the ground up.

    Talk to mvGPT

    ep003-sft · 8-bit · in your browser
      Features:

      A summary, or a few words to use (sun, kite, brave), and it writes a children's story. It doesn't answer questions, and each message is a new story. The first one downloads the model, about 15 MB, from Hugging Face.

      The beginning of a story, and it carries on. Six times the size, it trained for 30 minutes on four GPUs, on all of TinyStories, and wasn't fine-tuned, so it doesn't follow instructions. The first story downloads the model, about 92 MB, from Hugging Face.

      1. Build a GPT from scratch in PyTorch

        A small GPT written from scratch in PyTorch, from its parameters to how a prompt flows through it to the next token.

        32 min

      2. Pretraining a GPT from scratch in PyTorch

        From random tokens to short stories in a first training run on a laptop, with a tokenizer, the next-token objective, a training loop and evaluation written from scratch.

        27 min

      3. SFT: fine-tuning a GPT to follow instructions

        Our pretrained model learns to write the story an instruction asks for, with a loss on the story alone, and we measure how much better it follows instructions.

        27 min

      4. KV cache from scratch in PyTorch

        We add a KV cache to our model, so it runs each new token on its own and not the whole story again, and we check and time it on the CPU and the GPU.

        20 min

      5. Deploy a GPT: Hugging Face, Gradio, a browser

        We save our model as a GPT-2 and push it to the Hugging Face Hub, with a tag per episode, serve it from a Gradio app on a VPS, and run it in the browser with Transformers.js.

        28 min

      6. Profiling a GPT with PyTorch's profiler

        We look at our model as a graph of operations in Model Explorer, profile it with PyTorch's profiler, and measure how decisions in its code change its speed on a CPU and a GPU.

        18 min

      7. DPO: teaching a GPT from preferences

        With direct preference optimisation, our fine-tuned model learns from pairs of its own stories, where the one that uses more of the words asked for is preferred, and we measure what it gains and what it costs.

        17 min

      8. PPO: reinforcement learning for a GPT

        Our model learns from its own stories, scored as it writes them, with proximal policy optimisation, a critic and a penalty for drifting, and we measure it next to DPO.

        22 min

      9. LoRA: fine-tuning a GPT with 2% of its weights

        We fine-tune our model on instructions again with LoRA, which freezes its weights and trains a small low-rank update beside them, and measure what it saves and what it learns against full fine-tuning.

        15 min

      10. RoPE: rotary position embeddings from scratch

        We replace our model's table of positions with rotary position embeddings, which turn each query and key by its position, so attention scores depend on how far apart two tokens are, and the KV cache can slide past the context window.

        17 min

      11. BPE in C++: a byte-level tokenizer for a GPT

        We write a byte-level BPE tokenizer in C++, test it against a Python reference, pretrain our model on the stories in BPE, and compare the two tokenizers' models in bits per character.

        32 min

      12. Distributed training in PyTorch: 4 GPUs, DDP

        We pretrain a model about six times bigger, in GPT-2 small's shape, on four RTX 4090s rented by the hour, with PyTorch's DistributedDataParallel, and measure how the speed scales from one GPU to four, and how much better the model gets in thirty minutes.

        29 min

      13. Flash attention from scratch: PyTorch, Triton

        We write attention without its table of scores, first in PyTorch on the Mac, then as a Triton kernel on a rented NVIDIA GPU, forwards and backwards, and measure it against the FlashAttention-2 inside PyTorch.

        39 min

      14. Batching: serving a GPT to many users at once

        We serve the model as an API in the shape of OpenAI's completions, measure it against a crowd of players from a game, write the stories for the requests that arrive together as a batch, and run it in Docker, with vLLM as the alternative.

        25 min

      15. Text diffusion: a GPT that writes all at once

        We turn our GPT into a masked diffusion model, which writes a story by unhiding it, train it on the laptop and on four rented GPUs, start one from our next-token models' weights, and measure it against next-token prediction, comparing our code with LLaDA's, which trains this recipe at 8 billion parameters.

        34 min

      16. GRPO: reinforcement learning without a critic

        Our model writes eight stories per instruction, and each is scored against the others, in place of PPO's critic, with group relative policy optimisation, the reinforcement learning behind DeepSeek-R1.

        22 min

      17. RMSNorm, SwiGLU, QK-norm: a modern GPT block

        We bring our GPT's transformer block up to date with RMSNorm, SwiGLU, QK-norm and no biases, the changes recent open models made to it, each behind a config flag, with tests, and work out what they do to the parameter count.

        36 min

      18. Grouped-query attention: a smaller KV cache

        We give our model grouped-query attention, where the query heads share their keys and values in groups, so the KV cache keeps fewer heads, and we check it against the attention we had and work out what it saves.

        22 min

      19. Pretraining on the web: a GPT overnight

        We pretrain a model in GPT-2 small's shape on educational web pages for a whole night, on one RTX 4090 rented for under four dollars, and measure it against GPT-2 small on HellaSwag.

        27 min

      20. LLM tool calling from scratch: a calculator

        We fine-tune the model we pretrained on the web to call a calculator, in plain text, and measure how often it calls it right, on questions asked the way training asks and in new ways, with the calculator and without it.

        21 min

      21. AI agent from scratch, with subagents

        We fine-tune our web model into an agent that writes files and hands work to subagents, each a fresh context of the same model, and measure what the subagents buy.

        23 min

      22. Long context LLM: stretching RoPE with YaRN

        We stretch the rotary embeddings of our web model to read four times its window, four ways and then with fifteen minutes of fine-tuning, and measure the loss by position and a pass key hidden in filler.

        39 min

      23. Muon vs AdamW, Lion and Sophia: a fair race

        We write AdamW from scratch, then race it against Lion, Sophia and Muon on our stories model, each with its own sweep of the learning rate, and measure the steps, the time and the memory each needs.

        46 min

      24. Text to Cypher: our GPT asks a graph database

        We fine-tune our agent to write Cypher queries for smollerGraph, the graph database built in our sibling series, and to answer from what comes back, then measure it with the graph and without.

        27 min

      25. AI agent: a graph database and a calculator

        Our agent gets a fourth role, which queries smollerGraph in Cypher, so it can take two facts from a graph database and combine them with the calculator, and we measure what it kept of its old tasks.

        27 min

      26. Constrained decoding: structured LLM output

        Nothing is trained. Before each token inside a query's name, our agent's graph role masks the tokens that would take the name off a list, and we measure two lists, the graph's names and the task's words.

        25 min

      27. Speculative decoding from scratch

        A draft guesses the next few tokens, and our agent checks them in the pass that writes its own, so it writes what greedy decoding writes in fewer passes. Two drafts, prompt lookup and a small model trained for thirty minutes, measured on the agent's tasks.

        26 min

      28. Attention heads in our GPT, seen and tested

        We look inside our agent while it copies a name into a query, with each head's attention written out, the logit lens, and a page of our own, then turn the heads that look at the name off to see what they do.

        23 min
